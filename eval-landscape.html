How Models Get Measured

A model card is a vendor's own scorecard. The organisations below exist because that is not enough. This is who they are, what separates their numbers from a self-reported table, and how the MiniCPM5-2B reproduction we are running maps onto that standard.

Who measures what

Four distinct jobs, often confused. Capability ranking, buyer-facing comparison, autonomy measurement, and safety assurance are different questions and use different instruments.

Organisation What it publishes The method that matters
Epoch AIIndependent research Benchmarking Hub across roughly 80 benchmarks, the Epoch Capabilities Index, and FrontierMath including an open-problems tier. Runs a subset in-house on a fixed harness rather than trusting reported numbers. The Index infers each benchmark's difficulty statistically from models evaluated on overlapping sets, so a saturated benchmark still contributes. Anchored so Claude 3.5 Sonnet sits at 130 and GPT-5 at 150.
Artificial AnalysisCommercial analyst Intelligence Index, currently 10 evaluations, published alongside price, latency and throughput for each serving endpoint. Scores pass@1 aggregated over repeats, weighted by category rather than averaged flat: agents 30%, general 30%, coding 20%, scientific reasoning 20%. Publishes a confidence interval, stated as under ±1% at 95%.
METRAutonomy research Task-completion time horizon, reported in hours of human-expert task time rather than as a percentage. Measures the task length at which a model succeeds half the time, drawn from RE-Bench, HCAST and shorter software tasks. Converts capability into a unit a non-specialist can reason about, and has tracked a doubling roughly every four months.
UK AI Security InstituteGovernment Inspect, the open-source framework a large part of the field now writes evaluations in. Three abstractions carry everything: a dataset of samples, a solver that produces an answer, a scorer that grades it. Learning this structure is the single most transferable eval skill, because other groups ship their suites against it.
Apollo ResearchSafety Pre-deployment evaluations for strategic deception, evaluation awareness and misaligned goal pursuit. Tests whether a model behaves differently when it detects it is being tested, which is the failure mode that invalidates every other number on this page. Runs on Inspect.
Benchmark authorsAcademic and lab SWE-bench, τ²-bench, BFCL, LiveCodeBench, ARC-AGI, Terminal-Bench, MathArena and the rest of the rows on any model card. Each owns its own harness, scoring rules and contamination policy. Two parties reporting the same benchmark name have not necessarily run the same evaluation.

What separates a number from a claim

Six levers. Each one is a question to ask of any table you are handed, scored here against the MiniCPM5-2B card as an example you can walk through from memory.

One harness for every model in the table

Card fails

The two chat templates disagree on the default. MiniCPM5 leaves the reasoning block open for the model to fill; Qwen3.5 inserts a pre-closed empty one, which suppresses reasoning. Calling apply_chat_template() without an explicit flag runs the subject with reasoning and the baseline without it.

Repeats, and a stated interval

Card fails

Every cell is a bare point estimate. Artificial Analysis publishes an interval and says how many repeats produced it. On a 30-problem exam, the difference between two models can sit entirely inside the noise.

Contamination control

Card fails

Every model in the table scores higher on AIME 2026 than on AIME 2025. Newer exams should be harder to have memorised, not easier, so the ordering runs backwards from what a clean pipeline would produce.

Saturation handling

Card fails

MATH-500 runs from 85.4 to 99.0 across the table. A benchmark compressed into its top decile separates nothing, and averaging it in dilutes the rows that still discriminate.

An aggregate that reflects difficulty

Card fails

The headline 53.9 is an unweighted mean over 33 heterogeneous rows, which silently weights whichever category contributed the most rows. Artificial Analysis weights by category; Epoch fits difficulty from overlapping results instead of assuming it.

Third-party numbers, marked as such

Card passes

Daggered cells are attributed to Artificial Analysis rather than self-run. Mixing in independent numbers and labelling which ones they are is the one place this card behaves better than most.

This reproduction against that standard

Being explicit about what a small independent eval does and does not establish is itself the professional skill. Overstating coverage is how eval work loses its value.

What it establishes

  • One harness, both models. Same precision, sampler, prompt and reasoning flag, so a gap means something.
  • An ablation on the suspected confound. The baseline runs twice, reasoning on and off. If the off run lands near the card's number, the baseline column is explained.
  • Uncontaminated long-context data. The haystack is generated at run time, so no model can have seen it, and the needles need a world-knowledge hop rather than keyword matching.
  • Tests validated in both directions. Every buggy version was confirmed to fail and every reference fix to pass, so a score of zero means the model failed rather than the harness.
  • Per-sample records. Every generation is kept with its token count and truncation flag, so any headline number can be taken apart afterwards.

What it does not

  • Narrow intervals. Thirty problems at four samples leaves several points of noise. Enough to separate 30 from 80, not 84 from 88.
  • The actual SWE-bench claim. That needs per-repository containers. The bug-repair set is a proxy and has to be described as one.
  • Cost or latency. One machine, one backend, no serving comparison. Analysts treat this as half the question.
  • Agentic or tool-use rows. Untested here, and those are where the card's most unusual numbers sit.
  • Anything about safety. No deception, refusal or evaluation-awareness testing whatsoever.

Enhancing a small model for one use case

This is a genuine sequence, and the order is the point. Each rung costs more than the one above it, so you climb only until the measured gain stops paying. The first rung is not optional.

Build the held-out set before touching the model

Fifty to two hundred real examples from the actual use case, with a scorer you trust and have checked by hand. Split it and never look at the test half. Everything below is measured against this and nothing else.

Moves Nothing yet. Without it, no rung below can be shown to have worked.

Prompt and output contract

Constrain the shape of the answer, not just the task. For a 2B model, forcing structured output often recovers more than any weight change, because most failures are formatting and drift rather than reasoning.

Moves Format adherence and parse rate. Usually the largest single jump per hour spent.

Retrieval

Score the retriever separately from the generator. A drop in end-to-end accuracy that is really a recall problem will otherwise be misread as a model problem, and fine-tuning will not fix it.

Moves Knowledge coverage and factuality. Not reasoning.

Tool and scaffold design

Narrow the action space until the model can hit it reliably. Most agentic gains at this size come from removing choices rather than from a better model, which is why tool-use scores move so far on identical weights.

Moves Task completion, tool-call validity, error recovery.

Supervised fine-tuning, usually LoRA

Now you need a training split that is genuinely disjoint from the eval set, and a contamination check to prove it. This is the rung where teams most often fool themselves and report a gain they built into the test.

Moves Domain style, format, narrow-task accuracy. Rarely general capability.

Distillation from a larger teacher

Generate traces with a stronger model, train the small one on them. This is what MiniCPM5 says it did, and it is also why its benchmark profile looks unlike a normal 2B model. Powerful, and it optimises directly toward whatever the teacher was good at.

Moves Whatever the teacher was strong at, including benchmark-shaped behaviour.

Quantise and serve, then re-run everything

Run the same held-out set after quantising. This is where silent regressions live, and skipping it is the most common way a validated model gets worse in production without anyone noticing.

Moves Latency, memory and cost. Sometimes accuracy, downward, quietly.
